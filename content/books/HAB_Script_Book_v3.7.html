Clean Scripts for Roleplay & Memorization
Scripts only. No psychology, no callouts, no interruptions — just the exact words, with speakers labeled, one script per page. Use this book at the counter, in morning roleplay, and for memorization reps. The teaching behind every script lives in the HAB Advisor Book.

The one rule above every script: the goal of every inbound call is to get the car into the shop. Book the appointment. The sales process (secure the appointment) leads; the sales objectives (rapport, credibility, education) are earned through and after the close. The scripts serve the booking — never the other way around.

Version History

What's New Since Version 1

Your shop received Version 1 of this book. This edition replaces it. Here is everything that has changed, in plain language. Where this book and your memory of the old scripts disagree, this book wins.

The Quick Close now opens every inbound call. The call opens by answering the customer's question and closing for the visit in the same breath: "Absolutely, you've called the right place. Is now a good time to bring your vehicle in?" There is no shop introduction until the appointment is secured. If they can't come now, East-West scheduling offers two specific times.

The full inbound call ships in two versions. Version 1 for the customer who asks directly (answer and close immediately) and Version 2 for the price or quote inquiry (concern question, urgency line, no-charge line, then the close). Concern Discovery is a trained checkpoint on every rep.

Say Yes, Get Them In — No Pre-Education. The phone call has one job: say yes and get the vehicle in the door. Questions, urgency, and value framing are allowed. Warnings and caveats about whether the requested service will fix the problem are banned until the inspection produces evidence. Never say "not just [what they asked for]," and never justify the no-charge line — it stands on its own. The price barrier is met first with the warm "Oh, no no" beat.

Value is stated affirmatively — in every script. The "not just [what they asked for]" construction is banned in all customer-facing dialogue, in every script and every boss battle, not only on the phone. In the customer's mind "not just X" negates their request and hints at extra, and extra means extra cost. Every script states the value directly instead.

The Root Concern Rule. Even when the customer names a service and says yes, the advisor finds the TRUE root concern before writing the job — with questions, never with warnings.

The Keys Moment and the Audible. Intake was rebuilt. The real shop tour happens on the walk in (the inspection-rack line), and the waiting-area offer is replaced by the keys moment: "And you're dropping this off with me, right?" If the customer plans to wait, the audible surfaces the single most critical safety concern and shows it in person.

The DVI Timing Rule. Never book the appointment with the inspection — book with the customer's concern. The inspection is presented only after the concern is addressed or the first approval is secured, wrapped inside a waiting window.

The ISO total's "including" points backward. The investment line may reference only work the customer has already approved ("including the oil service you already approved"), never the repair being presented.

Reassurance opens on second and third base. Follow-up calls open by confirming the approved work is already moving, and everything new is labeled "additional." First base closes by acknowledging the yes, assuming the sale, and introducing the bumper to bumper inspection naturally.

Boss Battle 9 — The Second Opinion. The eight Boss Battles are now nine. The new battle counters "I want a second opinion / I can get it done cheaper" with the Honest Disarm, the Short Stop live play (the Short Stop reinforces the advisor first and never contradicts him), the Repair Plan, and the Customer-for-Life Close — with a full worked example.

Clean scripts only, one per page. This book is the roleplay companion: labeled speakers, no teaching interruptions. All of the psychology and coaching behind these scripts lives in the HAB Advisor Book.

Roleplay rules: One partner reads Customer, one reads Advisor. Swap roles every rep. Read at conversation pace, not reading pace. Stage directions in [brackets] are performed, not spoken. Bracketed placeholders like [Shop] and [Customer Name] get filled with real words, out loud, every time.

Trained checkpoint — Concern Discovery: in every inbound rep, the advisor must be able to state the customer's true concern in the customer's own words before the call ends. If you can't state it, you ask for it. The checkpoint is marked inside the scripts where it must happen.

Say Yes, Get Them In — No Pre-Education: the inbound call has ONE job: say yes to the customer and get them in the door. Questions are allowed. Urgency is allowed. Value framing is allowed ("at no cost to you, my certified technician and I will inspect your complete oil system and provide you a complete written estimate"). Never say "not just [what they asked for]" — and the ban is universal: the "not just X" construction is banned in ALL customer-facing dialogue, in every script in this book, because it subliminally tells the customer no, insinuates extra, and extra means extra cost. State the value affirmatively instead. The no-charge line needs no justification. Script 1 · Inbound Phone

The Full Inbound Call — Version 1 · Customer Ready (Direct Ask)

The customer asks directly for a service. Answer the question, close for the visit immediately, then branch. Never quote a price on the phone.

[Pick up before the third ring. Smile before you speak.]

Advisor: "Thank you for calling [Shop]. My name is [Your Name]." [Pause. Let the customer speak.]

Customer: "Hi, do you work on brakes?"

Advisor: "Absolutely, you've called the right place. Is now a good time to bring your vehicle in?"

Customer: "Yeah, I can head over now."

Advisor: "Great, have you been with us before?"

Branch A · First-time customer

Customer: "No, this will be my first time."

Advisor: "Thank you so much for choosing us! We are [Shop], your certified dealer alternative, located at [address], open six days a week from [store hours]. We have ASE and dealer-certified technicians ready to handle all of your vehicle needs, from tires and service to major repairs. We stand behind our work with warranties up to 3 years or 36,000 miles. We also offer complimentary shuttles, vehicle pick-up and drop-off, ride services, rental car reimbursement, and loaner vehicles when available! Stay with me really quick while I gather your information to build a customer profile."

[Gather name, number, email, vehicle year, make, and model.]

Advisor: "And so I can point our technician at exactly the right thing, what kind of concern are you having with your brakes?"

Customer: "They squeak when I stop."

Advisor: "Oh my goodness, when you have a squeak coming from your brake system, you've got to have it inspected by one of our ASE-certified technicians, so I'm glad you're coming in. Perfect, [Customer Name], you're all set. We'll see you and your vehicle shortly!"

Script 4 · Inbound Phone · Self-Diagnosis Defense

The Root Concern Rule — Self-Diagnosis Defense

Even when the customer names a service and says yes to coming in, find the TRUE root concern before writing the job. Discovery runs on questions, urgency, and value only. Never warn the customer on the phone that their fix may not work. Say yes, get them in. Education comes after the inspection produces evidence.
Version A · The named service (oil change)

Customer: "Can you do an oil change?"

Advisor: "Absolutely, you've called the right place. Is now a good time to bring your vehicle in?"

Customer: "Yeah, I can come in now."

Advisor: "Perfect. And real quick, what's going on with your oil system? Why are we doing an oil change?"

Customer: "I'm just overdue on the miles."

Advisor: "Got it, we'll get you right back on schedule. Have you been with us before?"

Same call, different answer · The light changes everything

Customer: "The oil pressure light came on."

Advisor: "Oh my goodness, when your oil pressure light comes on, you've got to bring it in and have it inspected by one of our certified technicians. Is right now a good time to bring it in?"

Customer: "I just want to make sure I know how much it costs first."

Advisor: [Warm, almost surprised. Meet the price worry, don't pitch at it.] "Oh, no no. At no cost to you, my certified technician and I will inspect your complete oil system and provide you a complete written estimate. Are you able to bring it in right now?"

If the customer pushes for a ballpark

Customer: "Can you just ballpark me how much the oil change will be?"

Advisor: "Yeah, absolutely. Our oil changes are on par with everybody else in town. But the reality is you've got an oil pressure light on, so I'd really prefer not to charge you anything. Come in, let me take a look at that oil light, we can determine the correct oil change that's really going to solve the problem, and I'll provide you a complete written estimate. That costs you nothing. What I really need from you right now is not money. I need you to bring the car in so we can take a look and start addressing your problem."

✔ Trained checkpoint · Root Concern — never write the job until you can state the true customer concern. If you can't state it, ask. Questions and urgency only; education waits for the inspection.
Version B · The customer-supplied diagnosis (engine)

Customer: "I've got a 2014 Chevy Silverado and my own engine. Can you guys remove the old one and install this new one?"

Advisor: "Absolutely, but let's back up for a second. Why do you need a new engine? What's happened, what's going on with the vehicle?"

Customer: "It started knocking real bad, so I found a replacement engine."

Advisor: "Oh my goodness, with a knock like that, you've got to bring it in and have it inspected by one of our certified technicians. At no cost to you, my certified technician and I will inspect your complete engine and vehicle and provide you a complete written estimate. Is now a good time to bring it in?"

[The inheritance reasoning stays in the advisor's head, never in the customer's ear: a customer-supplied part plus a customer-supplied diagnosis is double inheritance, and the shop never installs a fix for a diagnosis it hasn't confirmed. On the phone: questions, urgency, value. The evidence conversation happens after the inspection.]

Script 5 · Inbound Phone · Price Counters

The Four Inbound Mini-Bosses

Counter, then close for the visit. Never a number over the phone.
Mini-Boss 1 · "Just tell me how much it will cost."

Customer: "Yeah, I just want to know how much it will cost before I come in."

Advisor: "AT NO CHARGE TO YOU, my certified technician AND I will inspect your vehicle and provide you with a complete written estimate. Is now a good time to bring it in?"

Mini-Boss 2 · "I already have a quote."

Customer: "I have a quote already from another shop. That's why I'm just looking for a price."

Advisor: "Oh, excellent! If you can bring that with you, that would be tremendously helpful! We have a Meet-or-Beat policy that allows us to price-match like-for-like quotes if we don't naturally beat the price with our estimate. To give you the most accurate pricing, though, it's best if my certified technician and I take a look first to identify exactly what's needed to fix the [customer concern]. When should we expect to see the vehicle?"

Mini-Boss 3 · "Do you have a ballpark?"

Customer: "Do you have a ballpark for me? I just want to know roughly how much."

Advisor: "I completely understand! At no charge to you, my certified technician AND I will inspect your vehicle and provide a complete written estimate so you can make informed decisions. If budget is your primary concern, I will work with you to select cost-effective solutions that still carry good warranty coverage on parts and labor. As far as a ballpark, I can pull up a tool we use called RepairPal and give you an idea of what something like this normally costs to repair."

Mini-Boss 4 · "Let me think about it."

Customer: "I'm not sure yet. Let me think about it."

Advisor: "I completely understand. Before you decide, may I ask what's most important to you: price, turnaround time, or overall service?" [Listen carefully.]

Script 6 · In Person

The Approach + The Shop Tour

Every first-time customer. Walking pace, under 90 seconds, drink offer at the end.

[Greet at the front door or parking lot, boom board in hand.]

Advisor: "Welcome to [Shop]. I'm [Your Name] and I'll be assisting you today. May I have your keys, please?"

Customer: "Sure, here you go."

Advisor: "Thank you! Let me show you around while we get you checked in."

[Walking pace. Eye contact. Warmth.]

Advisor: "Welcome to [Shop], your certified dealer alternative. We're proud to have certified technicians ready to take care of all your vehicle needs: tires, routine maintenance, and major repairs. We also partner with trusted local specialists for accessory work like dent repair, body work, and windshield replacement, so you're covered in one place. To make your visit convenient, we offer complimentary shuttle rides, Uber or Lyft, rental car reimbursement, and loaner vehicles when available. If you stay, the waiting area has complimentary water, coffee, and tea, but there's no need to wait if transportation works better. Once you drop off, we keep you informed every step of the way: calls, texts, digital vehicle inspections, and electronic approvals for estimates, invoices, and payment. Clear, simple, transparent. We're excited to serve you today, and thank you for trusting [Shop]. Can I get you something to drink? Coffee, water, or tea?"

[Escort to the waiting lobby. Customer seated or comfortable before you step away.]

Script 7 · In Person

Custody — The Arrival & Intake

Keys first, concern documented, the real shop tour on the walk in, customer separated from the vehicle within 10 minutes. The keys moment closes the intake.

[Greet at the front door or parking lot, boom board in hand.]

Advisor: "Welcome to [Shop]. I'm [Your Name] and I'll be taking care of you today. May I have your keys, please?"

Customer: "Sure, here you go."

Advisor: "Thank you! Let me confirm what we have: you're here for [customer concern], and the best number to reach you is [number], correct?"

Customer: "That's right."

[Walk the customer inside past the inspection rack. Walking pace. This walk is the real shop tour.]

Advisor: "We keep this rack open so we can get you answers as soon as possible. This is also where we perform our bumper to bumper inspections, so you know the full health of your vehicle. It's not so I can present you an estimate later. It's so you have evidence and information about your car. At the end of the day we deliver safe and reliable vehicles."

Advisor: "While the vehicle is here, my certified technician and I will complete a full digital inspection with photos and video, I'll make sure the technician understands exactly what you're experiencing, and nothing happens without your approval. You'll see everything we see, and I'll personally walk you through the written estimate before anything moves forward."

Customer: "Sounds good."

[The keys moment. Hold the keys up.]

Advisor: "And you're dropping this off with me, right?"

If the customer plans to wait · The audible

Customer: "No, I was planning on waiting."

Advisor: "Oh, no worries. Can I get you a coffee, water, or tea?"

[Call the audible: the separate-the-customer-from-the-vehicle procedure begins. During the inspection, identify the one most critical safety concern, then return to the customer.]

Advisor: "Hey, we've identified something that requires your immediate attention. Do you mind coming to take a look at it with us?"

[At the vehicle: show them it is broken. Show and sell.]

Advisor: "We've got to have our master technician get this fixed so it's safe and reliable."

Customer: "How much is that going to cost?"

Advisor: "I don't know yet. We'll source the parts and I will call you before anything else moves forward."

[Boom board complete before the vehicle enters the bay. Customer separated from the vehicle within 10 minutes.]

Script 8 · Phone · First Base

The PRS Write-Up Verbal (First Base Call)

Isolate the customer's original concern. Problem, Risk, Solution, Investment, Coverage — no pause before Coverage.

Advisor: "Hi [Customer Name], this is [Your Name] with [Shop]. Is now a good time to review our findings?"

Customer: "Sure, go ahead."

Advisor: "You brought your vehicle in for [customer concern] and I inspected the vehicle with the technician personally to ensure accuracy. Good news is I was able to capture a good video of what is going on. Did you receive the DVI findings I sent via text?"

Customer: "Yes, I saw them."

Advisor: "Awesome. More good news. We've identified the cause of your concern. The problem is [Problem]. If it's left as it is, [Risk]. The proper solution is [Solution]. The total investment to take care of this today is $[XXX], and everything is covered by our 3-year, 36,000-mile nationwide warranty on parts and labor."

Customer: "Okay, let's take care of it."

Advisor: "Awesome. I'll go ahead and get those parts ordered so we can keep moving forward, and while we're waiting on the parts we'll complete the bumper to bumper inspection and I'll reach out with any additional concerns. Is this still the best number to reach you?"

Customer: "Yes it is."

Advisor: "Great."

[If an objection comes instead of the yes, handle it normally, then seal with the same close. Obtain authorization before advancing to second base. Hang up between bases.]

Script 9 · Phone · First Base Safety

The 10-Step ISO Presentation (Water Pump)

The canonical safety presentation. Structure never changes; only the technical content does.

Advisor: "Hi [Customer], this is [Advisor] from [Shop]. Thanks for your patience. Is now a good time to go over your vehicle?"

Customer: "Yes, now works."

Advisor: "I personally reviewed your vehicle with our certified technician so I could give you a clear picture of what's going on. Were you able to view the photos, videos, or the digital inspection report we sent over?"

Customer: "I did, yes."

Advisor: "You brought your vehicle in for an oil change. We've completed the initial inspection and I wanted to review what we found. The good news is your digital inspection report clearly documents everything with photos and notes so you can see exactly what we're seeing, and we caught this early, before it turned into a bigger issue."

Customer: "Okay."

Advisor: "More good news: during our inspection we found that your water pump is leaking, which is separate from your original visit and something we can take care of now before it leads to further damage. If left as is, a leaking water pump can lead to engine overheating, coolant loss, and eventually major engine damage. The proper repair is to remove and replace the water pump and complete a full cooling system service so everything is clean, sealed, and operating at the correct temperature. The total investment for today's work, including the oil service you already approved, is $1,695.46, parts and labor included, and this work is covered by our 3-year, 36,000-mile nationwide warranty on parts and labor."

Customer: "Alright, let's go ahead and do it."

Advisor: "Okay, great. I'll get the parts ordered, and while we're waiting on those we'll finish the bumper to bumper digital inspection and I'll reach out if anything else needs attention. Is this still the best number to reach you?"

Customer: "Yes, that number is best."

Advisor: "Perfect. I'll get everything moving and call you with an update."

Second Base — Additional Findings Call

Separate call after first base authorization. Open with reassurance: the approved work is in progress. Everything presented here is additional. Expand with inspection findings, most safety-critical first.

Advisor: "Hey [Customer Name], this is [Your Name] at [Shop]. We've completed the service inspection and we're in progress on everything we've already talked about. Plus, we've completed the full vehicle inspection and we have a couple of additional concerns that require your attention. Is now a good time to review the inspection findings?"

Customer: "Sure."

Advisor: "Did you have a chance to review the pictures and videos I sent over? I refreshed the report, so there's more on there now."

Customer: "I did."

Advisor: "Good news is your vehicle's overall health is in EXCELLENT condition! The suspension system is operating properly. However, the axle boot is leaking grease. It's not an immediate failure, but left unaddressed it can damage the joint. The proper repair is to replace the CV axle to ensure proper lubrication and protection. To address all the additional concerns, the updated total investment is $[XXX], and obviously everything is covered by our 3-year, 36,000-mile nationwide warranty on both parts and labor. With the [safety or customer concern] in progress, I can go ahead and order these parts and have the technician take care of everything at once. I'll call you with an updated ETA when the parts arrive."

Customer: "Okay, that works."

Advisor: "Great, we'll get the parts ordered right now. The last thing I'm going to do is check for any upcoming scheduled maintenance and call you back with the maintenance plan to protect this investment, so work doesn't build up like this again, and you know what to anticipate for your next visit!"

[Hang up. Reset. Third base is its own call.]

Script 11 · Phone · Third Base

Third Base — Scheduled Maintenance Call

Soft sell. By this call three or four conversations have already happened: open with in-progress reassurance, then the additional maintenance items. Calm tone, no urgency stacking. A real opt-out is part of the script.

Advisor: "Hi [Customer Name], this is [Your Name] with [Shop]. Everything we've talked about is in progress and moving right along. I'm calling back with that maintenance plan, a few additional maintenance items to review. Is now a good time?"

Customer: "Sure."

Advisor: "The remaining items fall into preventative maintenance. Your transmission and differential fluids are overdue or coming due within the next 10,000 miles based on mileage, so ideally we should address those two services today while we have the car. Addressing them now keeps the systems up to date and maintains the lifetime protection plan, leaving the only services due next visit [services due], and the service after that will be the [plan]. Addressing the items due will move your total investment for today's work from $[XXX] to $[XXXX]. These services are covered by the lifetime protection plan that keeps major system components like the [expensive part] covered for unlimited miles, so long as the services don't go overdue.
